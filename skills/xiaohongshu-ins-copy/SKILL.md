# Xiaohongshu & Instagram Copy

Turn source material into platform-native copy that is specific, credible, and recognizably on-brand. Preserve the user's facts and intent; do not make unsupported copy sound persuasive by making it more certain.

## Route the request

- For Xiaohongshu deliverables, read [references/xiaohongshu.md](references/xiaohongshu.md).
- For Instagram deliverables, read [references/instagram.md](references/instagram.md).
- When a brand voice is supplied, or the user asks for calm Chinese-inspired, crystal, tea, meditation, or sound-healing language, read [references/brand-voice.md](references/brand-voice.md).
- Read [references/examples.md](references/examples.md) only when examples would materially help match a format or voice. Treat them as patterns, never as source facts.
- When the user asks to test whether the Skill helps, create an A/B comparison, or evaluate variants, read [references/evaluation.md](references/evaluation.md).
- When the request supplies an Alibaba product ID, asks to choose from the real product
  library, or requires catalog-grounded copy, read
  [references/product-catalog.md](references/product-catalog.md).

Read only the references needed for the current deliverable.

## Build the working brief

Extract what is available:

- platform and deliverable;
- communication goal and intended reader;
- product, scene, or topic facts;
- proof, personal experience, and explicit unknowns;
- desired voice, language, length, call to action, and constraints.

When a configured catalog product ID is present, retrieve its grounded brief before drafting.
Do not rely on remembered product details or search by a guessed ID.

Do not block ordinary drafting for missing optional fields. Make the smallest reversible assumptions, disclose any assumption that changes the message, and leave factual gaps neutral. Ask only when a missing fact would materially change the claim or outcome.

## Protect factual boundaries

Use this evidence order:

1. facts and constraints in the current request;
2. an exact-ID product brief retrieved from the configured read-only catalog;
3. attached or linked source material actually inspected;
4. current project brand documentation;
5. stylistic conventions in this Skill.

Never invent prices, ingredients, specifications, locations, availability, customer results, certifications, testimonials, or personal experiences. Separate:

- **fact**: explicitly supplied or verifiable in the given source;
- **experience**: attributed to the person who actually reported it;
- **interpretation**: framed as a possibility, impression, or creative angle.

For wellness, crystal, meditation, skincare, food, or fitness content, avoid diagnosis, treatment, guaranteed outcomes, universal suitability, and fear-based pressure. Use ritual, sensory, symbolic, or personal-experience framing when that is what the source supports.

## Draft for the platform

Write natively for each platform instead of translating line by line. Preserve the same facts and message while adapting hook, pacing, CTA, and tags.

- Deliver one polished version by default.
- When the user asks to explore, compare, or choose, provide 2–3 variants with a meaningful difference such as story-led vs benefit-led—not cosmetic synonym swaps.
- For bilingual work, localize imagery, rhythm, and CTA; do not mirror Chinese syntax in English.
- When images are provided, make the copy complement visible information rather than redundantly narrating it. Never claim to see a detail that is not visible.

## Self-review before delivery

Check silently:

1. Every concrete claim traces to a source fact.
2. Required terms, length, format, CTA, and exclusions are satisfied.
3. The opening gives a real reason to continue without clickbait or anxiety.
4. The body contains specific value rather than stacked adjectives.
5. Platform conventions feel natural but do not overpower the brand.
6. The same sentence is not repeated across title, body, CTA, and tags.
7. Sensitive claims have appropriate uncertainty and boundaries.

Fix failures before responding.

## Present the result

Match the output to the requested deliverable. For a full post, normally provide:

- title or opening hook;
- body/caption with readable line breaks;
- CTA, only when appropriate;
- a small set of relevant tags;
- optional image-overlay text or alt text when requested.

Keep reasoning out of the copy. After the draft, mention assumptions or claim risks only when they affect safe use. Do not publish, schedule, or write to external systems without an explicit request and the relevant authorization.
