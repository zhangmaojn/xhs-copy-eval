# Instagram copy guide

Use this guide for feed captions, carousel copy, Reel captions, bios, and English or bilingual Instagram adaptations. Treat format conventions as defaults, not immutable platform rules.

## Caption architecture

The first visible lines should carry the central image, tension, or value. Then choose one compact shape:

- **Story**: moment → sensory detail → meaning → invitation.
- **Education**: useful claim → short explanation → actionable points → save/share prompt if earned.
- **Product**: scene → verified feature → experience or use → availability/CTA only if supplied.
- **Portfolio/editorial**: creative intent → selected detail → context/credit → response prompt.

Use short paragraphs for mobile reading. A poetic opening still needs a concrete anchor; an educational caption still needs a human voice.

## Carousel and Reel copy

### Carousel

- Cover promises one coherent idea.
- Each card advances it; avoid caption-sized paragraphs on the image.
- Caption adds context rather than duplicating every card.
- Add alt text when accessibility or discoverability matters and the image content is known.

### Reel

- On-screen hook must make sense without audio.
- Caption can supply context, steps, sources, or a reflective close.
- Do not invent what occurs in footage that has not been inspected.

## Hashtags and CTA

Use a small, relevant mix of subject, niche, audience/scene, and brand tags when tags help. Do not promise a fixed reach outcome or pad the caption with unrelated high-volume terms.

Choose the CTA that matches the post:

- conversation: a specific, answerable question;
- utility: save for later, only if the content is genuinely reusable;
- commerce: visit, inquire, or shop only when the destination and availability are supplied;
- brand: follow the series or explore a named collection when it exists.

## English localization

- Recreate the emotional function, not Chinese word order.
- Prefer plain, sensory English to translated abstractions.
- Keep imagery coherent; avoid stacking “healing,” “energy,” “soul,” and “ritual” without concrete meaning.
- Translate culture-specific terms briefly when the audience may not know them; retain the original term when it adds identity.
- Preserve all factual uncertainty and claim boundaries across languages.

## Bio

Build from identity + value/experience + optional proof or location + next action. Never invent credentials, founding dates, stockists, or shipping coverage. Keep within the current user-specified character limit and count before delivery.

## Final checks

- Do the first lines work before the caption is expanded?
- Is the copy additive to the image or carousel?
- Is the English idiomatic rather than literal?
- Are credits, facts, availability, and claims sourced?
- Is the CTA proportionate to the relationship and post goal?
