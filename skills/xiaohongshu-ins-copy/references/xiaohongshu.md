# Xiaohongshu copy guide

Use this guide for notes, titles, carousel text, comment prompts, and short shopping-guide copy intended for Xiaohongshu. These are writing conventions, not claims about a fixed ranking algorithm; follow current user or platform requirements when they differ.

## Choose the note's job

Pick one primary job before writing:

- **Search answer**: directly solve a named problem or question.
- **Experience record**: describe a real process, observation, or before/after state without inventing results.
- **Practical guide**: organize steps, choices, comparisons, or a checklist.
- **Aesthetic story**: build a scene and emotion while retaining useful specifics.
- **Product introduction**: connect verified features to a use scene without absolute promises.

Do not combine every job in one short note.

## Title

Aim for a clear reader–scene–value combination. Natural title patterns include:

- `[人群/场景] + [具体收获]`
- `[真实体验] + [克制结论]`
- `[问题] + [可执行答案]`
- `[清单/步骤/路线] + [适用条件]`

Prefer concrete nouns and observable details. Avoid manufactured urgency, unprovable superlatives, excessive punctuation, and titles that promise more than the body delivers.

## Body shapes

Select the smallest useful structure.

### Experience note

Scene → what was tried → specific observation → boundary/fit → gentle close.

### Guide or checklist

Problem → numbered steps → common mistake or condition → concise takeaway.

### Product note

Use scene → verified features → attributed personal experience → who may consider it / who should verify first → neutral CTA.

### Carousel

- Cover: one message, readable without the caption.
- Middle cards: one sub-point per card; headings should carry the logic.
- Final card: summary, question, or next action; do not repeat the cover verbatim.

## Search terms and tags

Place the primary phrase naturally in the title or early body when discoverability matters. Add only closely relevant supporting phrases. Use a restrained set of specific topic, scene, audience, or brand tags; avoid a wall of generic traffic tags. Never add a product, location, or identity tag unsupported by the brief.

## Tone

- Use short paragraphs and visible information hierarchy.
- Sound like a person with a concrete observation, not an advertisement pretending to be a diary.
- Emojis may mark rhythm or categories; they are optional and should not replace meaning.
- Prefer “我的体验是…” and “可以先确认…” over universal conclusions.
- A question CTA should invite a real response, not demand engagement.

## Short shopping-guide line

When a strict character limit is given, prioritize: core object + use scene + one verified distinction. Count characters before delivery. Do not squeeze disclaimers out if the remaining statement becomes misleading.

## Final checks

- Does the title match the body?
- Can every feature/result be traced to supplied material?
- Is personal experience clearly attributed?
- Are medical, skincare, food, crystal, and fitness claims appropriately bounded?
- Are tags relevant rather than decorative?
